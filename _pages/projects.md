<h2 class="paper-section">Dissertation chapters</h2>

<article class="paper">
  <h3 class="paper-title">Blame and Gain: Does Blame Attribution after a Natural Disaster Increase Support for the Incumbent? A German Case Study</h3>
  <p class="paper-meta"><span class="paper-tag paper-tag-solid">Dissertation chapter</span> <span class="paper-tag">In progress</span></p>
  <p class="paper-summary">Examines whether the way state governments frame a natural disaster in terms of blame attribution affects support for the incumbent. The study draws on plenary speeches from Germany's sixteen state parliaments between 2000 and 2022, and classifies blame strategies using large language models.</p>
  <p class="paper-presented">Presented at EPSS 2026 and PSAI 2026.</p>
</article>

<article class="paper">
  <h3 class="paper-title">Who Blames Whom after a Natural Disaster? Blame Attribution Strategies across Political Systems</h3>
  <p class="paper-meta"><span class="paper-tag paper-tag-solid">Dissertation chapter</span> <span class="paper-tag">In progress</span></p>
  <p class="paper-summary">Systematizes blame attribution theory and presents a large-scale comparative empirical study of how political actors attribute blame across different political systems.</p>
