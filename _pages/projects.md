<article class="paper">
  <h3 class="paper-title">Blame and Gain: Does Blame Attribution after a Natural Disaster Increase Support for the Incumbent? A German Case Study</h3>
  <p class="paper-meta"><span class="paper-tag">Work in progress</span></p>
  <p class="paper-summary">A difference-in-differences analysis of how German state governing parties attribute blame after natural disasters (externalizing, internalizing, credit-claiming, or staying neutral) and how that choice relates to their vote share at the next state election. Blame strategies are coded from parliamentary speech using a large language model classification pipeline.</p>
</article>

<article class="paper">
  <h3 class="paper-title">Who Blames Whom after a Natural Disaster? Blame Attribution Strategies across Political Systems</h3>
  <p class="paper-meta"><span class="paper-tag">Work in progress</span></p>
  <p class="paper-summary">A typology of blame-attribution strategies, paired with a fine-tuned open-source large language model pipeline for classifying parliamentary speech at scale. Applied across eight cases spanning parliamentary and presidential systems: Germany, Greece, Spain, Sweden, Brazil, Chile, Mexico, and two U.S. states.</p>
</article>
