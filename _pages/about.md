I am a PhD candidate in Political Science at Trinity College Dublin. My research asks who gets blamed when a crisis hits, and what politicians do about it. I study how governments and opposition parties attribute responsibility for natural disasters and other crises, and whether the strategies they choose, such as shifting blame, claiming credit, or accepting responsibility, shape electoral outcomes. I use quantitative methods and computational text analysis for my research.

Before coming to Dublin, I completed my M.A. in Political Science at the University of Mannheim. I have also worked as a research assistant on the Comparative Study of Electoral Systems (CSES) at GESIS - Leibniz Institute for the Social Sciences, in Mannheim. Currently, I am a Teaching Assistant for under- and postgraduate modules at Trinity College.

### Research interests

- 💻 Computational methods
- 📊 Causal inference & quasi-experimental design
- 🫵 Attribution theory & blame in politics
- 🇬🇷 Greek politics
- 📣 Political communication
- 🌍 Environmental politics
